# Website and image reference register

Reviewed: 2 October 2026. All user-supplied URLs are retained below. Live sites can change; attached screenshots represent the user's chosen reference versions. Observed features and proposed High4Tech adaptations are separated. These are short original review notes, not copied site content.

## Existing High4Tech website

Source: [high4tech.framer.ai](https://high4tech.framer.ai/). Reviewed directly in the browser after the text-retrieval tool could not access it.

Observed: white/light surfaces, large introduction, contact CTA, project previews, a creative toolbox, four-step process, testimonials, FAQs, and social/contact areas. Current branding identifies a design and development agency. Retain useful information and project references while rebuilding the visual experience.

Content issues to confirm: upper sections show 45+ clients and 95+ projects, while a lower strip shows 60+ clients and 100 projects. Displayed sales@high4tech.io email links lead to a different template email. Some footer social links are generic or point to another identity; the booking link is the general cal.com homepage. Existing project titles and URL slugs do not match. These are cleanup findings, not permission to change the live site or evidence that the business claims are false.

## Displace

Source: [displace.agency](https://displace.agency/).

Observed visually: desktop-OS framing, a dark windowed interface, orange/red accents, dock icons, and a structured agency overview. Its text also describes playful app-like content destinations.

Proposed use: borrow restrained interactive personality and visual depth if useful. Do not make OS windows or a dock the default navigation without a later decision; High4Tech visitors still need clear routes to services and work.

## Fahrenheit

Source: [fahrenheit.ch](https://www.fahrenheit.ch/).

Observed visually: large serif headlines, pale warm background, generous spacing, and oversized pale graphic forms. Page content includes portfolio, capabilities, agency information, and contact.

Proposed use: adopt the editorial spacing and deliberate hierarchy. Treat its typography as inspiration rather than a requirement to match its font.

## Direct

Source: [wearedirect.co](https://wearedirect.co/).

Observed visually: slim black header, large black headline on white, left-side 3D/video graphic, and bright yellow/green brand accents. Content includes selected work, capabilities, principles, testimonials, and contact.

Proposed use: combine memorable 3D composition with crisp navigation and readable copy. Use High4Tech orange rather than Direct's palette or traffic-light identity.

## Marino

Source: [marino.co.uk](https://marino.co.uk/).

Observed: airy light hero, large typography, pill-shaped navigation/contact treatment, case studies, service categories, proof, articles, and FAQs.

Proposed use: keep conversion paths, services, and proof easy to scan. Never import its revenue figures, partner status, clients, or testimonials as High4Tech content.

## MadamePolare

Source: [madamepolare.com/en](https://madamepolare.com/en).

Observed visually after its loader: oversized wordmark, large editorial headline interspersed with image shapes, monochrome interface, and rounded action treatments.

Proposed use: expressive type/image composition and a strong sense of personality. Avoid its branding, exact words, and a mandatory percentage-loading introduction.

## Copula

Source: [copula.agency](https://copula.agency/).

Observed visually: saturated orange hero, condensed oversized typography, graphic accent shapes, and a prominent contact treatment. Content covers capabilities, featured work, clients, about, and news.

Proposed use: typographic energy and orange graphic accents within High4Tech's predominantly white layout. Its full-orange hero and blue accents are not the proposed High4Tech surface hierarchy.

## Tkxel

Source: [tkxel.com](https://tkxel.com/).

Observed live: blue hero, clear navigation, prominent service/resource paths, and enterprise-oriented content. The user-supplied long screenshot shows a different hero version, grouped services, proof, success stories, recognition, partners, insights, and a detailed footer.

Proposed use: content hierarchy and service/resource clarity from the attached reference. Avoid adding unsupported enterprise credentials or every section in its larger sitemap. Translate blue visual treatments to the High4Tech palette.

## Stōkt Heron case study

Source: [Heron — Stōkt](https://wearestokt.com/case-study-collection/heron). Added after the user confirmed white as the dominant website surface.

Observed visually: a large hanging badge composition on a pale field, generous margins, black editorial type, small orange section labels, and an introduction split between a headline and supporting text. The page organizes project metadata, vision, responsive examples, a gallery, and a testimonial. Some video playback was unavailable during inspection; motion was not fully verified.

Proposed use: white-dominant case studies with a strong visual opening, clear metadata, spacious storytelling, and large imagery. Adapt its pale/cream surfaces to the user's white preference. Borrow the composition and pacing rather than its badge artwork, fonts, client facts, or testimonial. A hanging-card interaction could be explored later only if it suits a High4Tech project.
